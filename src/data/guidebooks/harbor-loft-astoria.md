# Harbor Loft Astoria

Welcome to Harbor Loft, a one-bedroom loft on the top floor of a converted
cannery building on the Astoria waterfront in Oregon. Everything you need to
know about the place is below.

## Check-In and Check-Out

Check-in is any time after 3:00 PM. Check-out is 10:00 AM.

The building's street door uses a shared entry code, and the loft itself has a
smart lock. Both codes are sent by text the morning of your arrival and stop
working at 10:00 AM on the day you leave. The loft is unit 4B, up two flights —
there is a lift, but it is slow and noisy, and guests usually prefer the stairs.

Early check-in and late check-out depend on whether the loft is booked either
side of you. Ask the host the day before and they will tell you honestly.

## House Rules

This is a **no-pets** building. The strata rules do not allow animals in guest
units, including small dogs and cats, and there is no exception for service
animals staying overnight without prior written arrangement through the host.

No smoking or vaping anywhere in the building, including on the balcony. The
smoke detectors are sensitive and a false alarm calls out the whole building.

Quiet hours are 10:00 PM to 7:00 AM. Sound carries badly between units through
the old floors, so be mindful with music and with the stairs late at night.

Maximum occupancy is two guests. The loft has one bed and no sofa bed, and the
booking terms do not allow additional overnight visitors.

## Parking and Getting Here

The address is 220 Cannery Pier Way, Astoria, OR 97103.

You have **one assigned parking space, number 7**, in the garage beneath the
building. The garage entrance is on the north side and uses the same code as the
street door. Do not park in any other numbered space — they belong to residents
and are towed without warning.

Astoria is about two hours from Portland. The last stretch crosses the Astoria
bridge, which is worth the drive in daylight if you can manage it.

## Kitchen

The kitchen has an **induction hob and a dishwasher, but no oven** — this
surprises people, so plan meals accordingly. Induction needs magnetic pans, and
the ones provided are correct; your own may not work.

Coffee is a pour-over setup with filters in the drawer beneath. There is no
espresso machine and no kettle other than the stovetop one.

Dishwasher tablets, salt, pepper, oil and basic spices are provided. Start the
dishwasher before you leave.

## Heating and Cooling

Heating and cooling both run from the **mini-split unit** on the main wall.
The remote lives in the drawer of the console table. Set it and leave it —
the unit is slow to respond and cycling it makes the loft less comfortable, not
more.

There is no separate air conditioning. Astoria rarely needs it, and the sea
breeze through the two windows handles most warm evenings.

The bathroom has a heated towel rail on its own switch by the door.

## Trash and Recycling

There is a **rubbish chute on every floor**, in the alcove by the lift. Bag
everything before it goes down.

Recycling does not go down the chute. Take it to the labelled bins in the
basement, next to the garage entrance. Astoria separates glass from everything
else, and the bins are marked.

There is no collection day to worry about — the building handles it.

## Local Recommendations

For dinner, the Bridgewater Bistro by the pier does seafood well and takes
bookings. Fort George Brewery on Duane Street is louder, has no bookings, and is
better for a casual evening.

Coffee is Street 14 on Commercial, five minutes' walk.

The Columbia River Maritime Museum is the thing to do if you have one morning.
The Astoria Column is the thing to do if you have one afternoon and do not mind
164 steps. The Riverwalk runs right past the building — turn left out of the
door for the working waterfront, right for the quieter end.

## Emergency and Contact

For any emergency — fire, medical, or otherwise — call **911** first. The
address is 220 Cannery Pier Way, unit 4B. Tell dispatch it is the old cannery
building on the waterfront.

Your host is **Rosa Delgado**, reachable at **(503) 555-0188**. Text is fastest
and is answered from 8:00 AM to 9:00 PM. For an urgent problem outside those
hours — no heat, no water, a leak, or you are locked out — call rather than text.

The nearest hospital is Columbia Memorial in Astoria, on Exchange Street, about
five minutes away by car. It has a 24-hour emergency department.

If the power goes out, the torch is in the console drawer with the remote, and
the building's breaker panel is in the basement by the recycling. The lift will
not run during an outage, so use the stairs.

There is a fire extinguisher under the kitchen sink and a first aid kit in the
bathroom cabinet. The smoke and carbon monoxide detectors are hardwired; if one
starts chirping, tell the host rather than removing the battery.
